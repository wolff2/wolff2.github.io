What to Do in the First 48 Hours After a Parent Dies

Updated November 10, 2026 · LumivexaStore

If you are reading this because someone close to you has just died, we are sorry. The first days are overwhelming, and it can feel as if everything must be done at once. It does not. Only a few things truly need to happen in the first 48 hours. Most of the rest can wait a week or longer.

This guide is general information to help you get organised. It is not legal, tax or financial advice. Rules differ between states and countries, so check with a local probate lawyer or the court if you are unsure.

1. Get a legal pronouncement of death

If the death happens in a hospital or care home, staff will usually handle this. If it happens at home and the person was in hospice care, call the hospice. If the death was unexpected, call the emergency number. A pronouncement is needed before the funeral home can take the person into their care and before a death certificate can be issued.

2. Tell close family and friends

Start with the people who need to hear it personally. It is fine to ask one or two others to help pass on the news, so you are not making every call yourself.

3. Arrange care for anyone who depended on them

Children, a spouse who needs support, and pets need someone right away. Make sure they are safe and looked after before anything else.

4. Secure the home and the car

Lock the home, take valuables or important papers somewhere safe, and arrange for someone to collect mail and water plants. An empty home can attract theft, especially if a death notice is published.

5. Look for the will and any funeral wishes

Check for a will, a letter of wishes or a prepaid funeral plan. These may say who the executor is and what kind of funeral the person wanted. Do not distribute any belongings yet, even if family members ask.

6. Contact a funeral home

The funeral home will collect the person, explain your options and usually help you order certified copies of the death certificate. You do not need to decide every detail on the first call.

7. Tell their employer, if they were working

Ask about final pay, any life insurance or death benefits through work, and pension or retirement accounts.

What can wait

  • Closing bank accounts, cancelling subscriptions and notifying most companies
  • Applying to the court or probate office
  • Paying debts or bills from the estate
  • Selling or giving away belongings

These belong to the following weeks. Write them down so you do not need to hold them in your head, and let the first two days be about the person and your family.

A note for executors

If you have been named executor, avoid paying the estate's debts from your own money and keep every receipt for costs you do pay, such as funeral expenses. You may be able to be reimbursed from the estate later.
