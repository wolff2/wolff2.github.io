Who to Notify When Someone Dies: A Complete Checklist

Updated November 26, 2026 · LumivexaStore

After a death, a surprising number of people and organisations need to be told. You do not have to contact them all at once. This checklist puts them in a sensible order, so the urgent calls happen first and the rest can be worked through over the following weeks.

This guide is general information to help you get organised. It is not legal, tax or financial advice. Rules differ between states and countries, so check with a local probate lawyer or the court if you are unsure.

Before you start: get certified death certificates

Many organisations will ask for a certified copy of the death certificate before they act. The funeral home can usually order these for you. Ordering several copies at once is usually cheaper and faster than going back for more later.

In the first few days

  • Close family and friends. Ask one or two people to help pass on the news.
  • Their employer, if they were working. Ask about final pay, life insurance through work and any death benefits.
  • A funeral home. They will guide you through the next steps.
  • Their doctor and any care providers, so appointments and home visits stop.
  • The executor named in the will, if that is not you.

Government offices

  • Pension and social security benefits. In the US, the funeral home usually reports the death to the Social Security Administration, but confirm it has been done. Payments received after the death may need to be returned.
  • In the UK, the free Tell Us Once service lets you report a death to several government departments in one go.
  • Tax office, for the final tax return.
  • Driving licence, passport and voter registration, so documents can be cancelled.

Money and property

  • Banks and building societies. Accounts in their sole name are usually frozen until the estate is settled. Joint accounts may pass to the other owner.
  • Credit card companies and lenders: mortgage, car loan, personal loans. Ask them to stop interest and fees while the estate is dealt with.
  • Credit reporting agencies. In the US you can ask Equifax, Experian and TransUnion to flag the record as deceased, which helps prevent identity theft.
  • Pension and retirement plan providers, which may pay benefits to a spouse or named beneficiary.
  • Investment and brokerage accounts.

Insurance

  • Life insurance, to start a claim.
  • Health insurance, to end cover and settle final bills.
  • Home and car insurance. Keep an empty home and any car insured until they are sold or transferred.

The home and everyday services

  • Landlord or mortgage lender.
  • Utilities: electricity, gas, water, phone, internet and TV.
  • The post office, to redirect mail to the executor.
  • Regular deliveries and services: subscriptions, memberships, cleaners, gardeners.

Online accounts

  • Email and social media. Many platforms let family close an account or turn it into a memorial page.
  • Streaming services, apps and online shopping accounts with saved payment cards.

Keep a simple log

For every call or letter, write down who you spoke to, the date, any reference number and what happens next. When a bank calls back three weeks later, you will know exactly where things stand.
