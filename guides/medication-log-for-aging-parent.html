How to Keep a Medication Log for an Aging Parent

Updated November 13, 2026 · LumivexaStore

Older adults often take several medicines, prescribed by different doctors and filled at different times. A single, up-to-date medication log helps everyone involved: the family members who help with doses, the doctor who needs an accurate list, and your parent, who no longer has to remember everything.

This guide is about organisation, not medical advice. Always follow the instructions of your parent's doctor and pharmacist.

What to record for each medicine

  • Name of the medicine and the strength (for example, 20 mg)
  • Form: tablet, capsule, drops, inhaler, patch
  • What it is for, in plain words
  • When it is taken: morning, midday, evening, bedtime, or as needed
  • Who prescribed it, and which pharmacy fills it
  • The next refill date
  • Status: active, or stopped (and the date it was stopped)

Include everything, not only prescriptions

Vitamins, supplements, eye drops, creams and over-the-counter painkillers all belong on the list. Doctors and pharmacists use the full list to check for interactions, so a complete log is more useful than a neat one.

Do not delete stopped medicines

When a medicine is stopped, mark it as stopped instead of deleting the row. A history of what was tried, and when, is often exactly what a new specialist asks for.

Track refills before they run out

Running out of a regular medicine on a weekend is stressful and avoidable. Write the next refill date next to each medicine and check the list once a week. Anything due in the next seven days goes on that week's to-do list, with a name next to it.

Share it with the people who need it

  • Family members who give doses or pick up prescriptions should see the same current list.
  • Doctors: bring a printed copy to every appointment and ask the doctor to confirm it is correct.
  • Emergencies: keep a printed copy with your parent's emergency information, for example on the fridge or in a grab bag.

Review it regularly

Many pharmacists offer medication reviews, and doctors can check whether every medicine is still needed. Bring the log to these conversations. An accurate list makes the review faster and safer.
